How the math works

Each year until 18, we add your money and let it grow by the return you picked, minus the fund fee. After 18, nothing new is added and the balance keeps growing.

Details: the formula, the Dell $250 and taxes

Each year until the child turns 18: balance = (balance + family + employer) × (1 + return − fee). The starting balance includes the $1,000 Treasury seed and/or the Dell $250 deposit if ticked.

Dell $250: The Dell $250 went to eligible children born Jan 1, 2016 – Dec 31, 2024 living in ZIP codes with a median family income of $117,300 or less (U.S. Census 2020–2024 ACS), plus all eligible children on military bases; all deposits were made by October 9, 2026. It was automatic. Some children in higher-income ZIPs also got it because their families enrolled before auto-enrollment began; funds are reserved for children in foster care, expected next year. (Source: Invest America, figures as of Oct 9, 2026; verified Oct 27, 2026.)

After-tax estimate. At 18 the account follows traditional IRA rules. Money the family put in was already taxed, so it is your basis (not taxed again). The seed, the Dell gift, employer money and all growth are taxed as income when taken out. The calculator applies your tax rate to that part and, if ticked, a 10% penalty before 59½. Real taxes depend on the year, your state and exceptions.

Frequently asked questions

Does the calculator include the $1,000 seed?

Yes, if you tick the seed box. Only U.S. citizens born January 1, 2025 through December 31, 2028 qualify, and a parent must elect it on Form 4547.

What return should I use?

There is no guaranteed return. Broad U.S. stock indexes have historically averaged roughly 10% nominal per year over long periods, with large losses in some years. Many planners use 5–7% to be conservative.

Is the Trump Account tax-free?

No. It is tax-deferred, not tax-free. Growth is taxed as ordinary income when withdrawn (after 18), except the after-tax family contributions (basis).

Does a Trump Account Affect FAFSA?

By j6 · Updated October 28, 2026 · Facts from IRS and Treasury sources (listed below) · Not tax advice

Short answer: no one knows yet. The U.S. Education Department has not said how to report a Trump Account on the FAFSA. FAFSA rules leave out retirement accounts like IRAs, and a Trump Account is legally a traditional IRA. So some experts think it will not count. Others think it will count as the student's money, which could cut need-based aid by up to 20% of the balance.

Do not skip the free $1,000 over this. Even in the worst case, the aid you lose is smaller than the account itself.

Withdrawals can count as income

Taxable money taken out in college counts as the student's income on a later FAFSA, which can reduce aid. One planning idea quoted by CNBC: wait to withdraw until Jan 1 of the student's sophomore year, so the income falls outside the years FAFSA looks at. Check this against current FAFSA rules before acting.

What to do now

  • Claim the account and the $1,000 if eligible.
  • If college is the main goal for extra savings, a parent-owned 529 plan gets better aid and tax treatment.
  • Watch studentaid.gov for Trump Account guidance. We will update this page when it appears.

Frequently asked questions

Does a Trump Account count on the FAFSA?

Federal Student Aid has not issued guidance yet. Because a Trump Account is a traditional IRA, current FAFSA instructions excluding non-education IRAs suggest it may not be reported, but this is unconfirmed.

How much financial aid could a Trump Account cost?

If it were treated as a student investment asset, up to 20% of the balance per year in need-based aid (for example, up to $2,000 on a $10,000 account), according to Mark Kantrowitz via CNBC.

Should I skip the $1,000 seed to protect financial aid?

Experts quoted by CNBC say to claim it: in the worst case you would not lose more aid than the value of the account.

Do Trump Account withdrawals affect financial aid?

Taxable withdrawals add to the student's income, which can reduce aid on a later FAFSA.

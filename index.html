Trump Account Calculator & Guide (2026)

Written by j6 · Updated October 27, 2026 · Based on IRS/Treasury guidance · Not tax advice

A Trump Account is a tax-advantaged investment account for U.S. children created by the 2025 tax law (Internal Revenue Code section 530A). It is legally a type of traditional IRA for a child under 18. Family and others can add up to $5,000 a year (employers up to $2,500 of that), the money must sit in low-cost U.S. stock index funds (fees of 0.10% or less), and it generally cannot be withdrawn until the year the child turns 18. Children born 2025–2028 who are U.S. citizens can get a one-time $1,000 from the Treasury.

Quick answer: the $1,000 seed alone grows to about $3,363 by age 18 at a 7% return. Adding $1,000 a year from birth gives about $39,627 by 18. The maximum ($5,000/yr) gives about $184,684. Use the calculator for your own numbers.

Frequently asked questions

What is a Trump Account?

A Trump Account is a tax-advantaged investment account for U.S. children created by the 2025 tax law (Internal Revenue Code section 530A). It is legally a type of traditional IRA for a child under 18. Family and others can add up to $5,000 a year (employers up to $2,500 of that), the money must sit in low-cost U.S. stock index funds (fees of 0.10% or less), and it generally cannot be withdrawn until the year the child turns 18. Children born 2025–2028 who are U.S. citizens can get a one-time $1,000 from the Treasury.

How much will $1,000 in a Trump Account be worth at 18?

At a 7% annual return (before a 0.03% fee), the $1,000 Treasury seed alone grows to about $3,363 by age 18. Returns are not guaranteed.

What is the Trump Account contribution limit?

$5,000 per child per year from family and others, of which up to $2,500 can come from an employer. The $1,000 Treasury seed, qualified government/charity contributions and rollovers do not count toward the limit. The limit is indexed to inflation after 2027.

When can the money be withdrawn?

Generally not before January 1 of the year the child turns 18. After that the account follows traditional IRA rules: earnings and pre-tax money are taxed as ordinary income when withdrawn, and withdrawals before age 59½ may face a 10% penalty unless an exception (such as qualified higher education or a first home) applies.

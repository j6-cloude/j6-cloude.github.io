Trump Account Pros and Cons: What's the Catch?

By j6 · Updated October 28, 2026 · Facts from IRS and Treasury sources (listed below) · Not tax advice

Short answer: it costs nothing to claim, and the $1,000 is free. So claim it. The catches come later. The money is locked until 18. Most of it is taxed when it comes out. At 18 your child gets full control. And for now there is just one fund.

Pros

  • Free money: $1,000 for U.S. citizens born 2025–2028. Possible employer, charity and state gifts.
  • No fees to claim or keep it. The fund fee is 0.02% now (legal max 0.10%).
  • No yearly tax on growth until money comes out.
  • No earned income needed, unlike a custodial IRA.
  • Simple: one low-cost S&P 500 fund.
  • Family money comes out tax-free (it is basis).

The catches (cons)

  • Locked until 18. No emergency withdrawals.
  • Tax-deferred, not tax-free. Growth, the seed and employer money are taxed as income later. Many withdrawals before 59½ also owe 10%.
  • Your child controls it at 18. You cannot stop them from spending it.
  • No contribution deduction for family money.
  • One fund only for now, all in U.S. stocks. It can fall in value.
  • Can't be closed once claimed (except in rare cases).
  • First to claim wins. Between separated parents, whoever finishes the claim first manages it.
  • FAFSA effect is unknown (see FAFSA guide).
  • Scams copy the program. Real notices come only from the official app, trumpaccount.com, or no-reply@trumpaccounts.treasury.gov.

Does it really grow?

Treasury's own history check: $1,000 put in at birth in a broad U.S. stock index was worth $6,180 at 18 in the middle case and $2,980 in a bad (10th percentile) case, for children born 1926–2006. See investment options.

Frequently asked questions

What's the catch with Trump Accounts?

The money is locked until 18, most of it (growth, seed, employer money) is taxed as income when withdrawn, the child controls it at 18, there is only one fund for now, and the financial aid treatment is not yet settled.

Is a Trump Account worth it?

Claiming is free and the $1,000 seed (for eligible children) is free money, so claiming is generally worth it. Whether to add your own money depends on your goal: 529s are usually better for college and custodial Roth IRAs for teens with jobs.

Are there fees on Trump Accounts?

There is no fee to claim or maintain the account. Funds may charge up to 0.1%; the current fund, SPYM, charges 0.02%.

Can I close a Trump Account?

Once claimed it generally cannot be closed until 18, except in limited cases such as the child's death or an ABLE rollover.

Is the Trump Account a scam?

No, the program is real and run under IRS and Treasury rules, but scammers imitate it. Real activation emails come only from no-reply@trumpaccounts.treasury.gov.

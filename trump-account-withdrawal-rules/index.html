Trump Account Withdrawal Rules: What Happens at 18

By j6 · Updated October 28, 2026 · Facts from IRS and Treasury sources (listed below) · Not tax advice

Short answer: no one can take money out before the year your child turns 18. Then the account turns into a normal traditional IRA in your child's name. Your child, not you, controls it. They can take money out, but part of it is taxed as income. Most withdrawals before age 59½ also owe a 10% extra tax. Some uses skip that 10%, like college costs or a first home (up to $10,000).

Key facts
  • Last year to add money: the year your child turns 17 (by Dec 31).
  • From Jan 1 of the year they turn 18: IRA rules apply.
  • At 18 your child signs in and takes control. You lose access.
  • If no one ever claimed the account, it still becomes your child's IRA. The money is not lost.

Worked example

Say the account holds $25,000 at 18. Family put in $9,000 of their own (already-taxed) money. Your child takes out $5,000 for rent, at a 10% tax rate.

Amount
Tax-free part (36% of the account is family money)$1,800
Taxable part$3,200
Income tax at 10%$320
10% extra tax (rent is not an exception)$320
Kept$4,360

If the same $5,000 paid for college, the 10% extra tax would not apply and your child would keep $4,680. The tax-free share follows the IRA pro-rata rule. A Trump Account is never combined with your child's other IRAs for this rule (Notice 2025-68).

What can come out before 18?

Almost nothing. During the growth period the law allows only these moves:

  • A rollover of the whole balance to another Trump Account provider (trustee to trustee).
  • A rollover to an ABLE account (for a child with a disability) in the year the child turns 17.
  • Return of money put in over the $5,000 limit.
  • Payment after the child's death.

There is no hardship or emergency withdrawal before 18, unlike some other IRAs.

Which uses skip the 10% extra tax after 18?

The usual IRA exceptions apply. Common ones: qualified higher-education costs, up to $10,000 (lifetime) for a first home, and a few more listed by the IRS in Topic 557. Income tax still applies to the taxable part. See the full list at the IRS link below.

What happens if my child leaves it alone?

It keeps growing tax-deferred, like any IRA. After 18 your child can add more only under normal IRA rules, which need earned income (a job).

Frequently asked questions

When can my child withdraw money from a Trump Account?

Starting January 1 of the year the child turns 18. Before that, withdrawals are not allowed except for a full rollover, an ABLE rollover in the year the child turns 17, removing excess contributions, or death.

Who controls a Trump Account at 18?

The child. The account becomes a traditional IRA in their name; they set up their own login and the parent (responsible party) loses access.

Is there a penalty for withdrawing from a Trump Account?

After 18, the taxable part of a withdrawal before age 59½ generally owes a 10% additional tax unless an IRS exception applies, such as qualified higher-education costs or up to $10,000 for a first home. Income tax applies to the taxable part either way.

Can you contribute to a Trump Account after 18?

Not as a Trump Account. Contributions stop after December 31 of the year the child turns 17. After the account becomes a traditional IRA, the child can add money only under normal IRA rules, which require earned income.

What happens if nobody claims the account?

It stays invested. At 18 it converts to a traditional IRA in the child's name and the child verifies their identity to take control.

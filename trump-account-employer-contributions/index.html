Trump Account Employer Contributions

By j6 · Updated October 28, 2026 · Facts from IRS and Treasury sources (listed below) · Not tax advice

Short answer: an employer can put up to $2,500 a year into your children's Trump Accounts without it counting as income for you. The $2,500 is per worker, not per child. It counts toward each child's $5,000 limit. Over 50 companies have said they will offer it. Ask HR if yours does.

The rules in plain words

  • Limit: $2,500 per employee per year, in total for all your kids. It rises with inflation after 2027.
  • Tax: left out of your federal income tax. Proposed IRS rules say it still counts as wages for Social Security and Medicare (FICA) tax unless another rule excludes it.
  • Your own pay: an employer can let you put part of your pay in before tax through a cafeteria (Section 125) plan, but only for your dependent's account, not your own.
  • Later: employer money is taxed as your child's income when withdrawn. It does not create basis.
  • Plan needed: the employer must have a separate written plan, give notices and send yearly statements.
  • Self-employed? Owners such as sole proprietors and partners can set up a plan for staff but cannot use it themselves (proposed rules).

If you have two kids

The $2,500 cap covers both. The employer could put $1,250 in each, or $2,500 in one. Each child's own $5,000 cap still applies to everything added to that child.

Frequently asked questions

How much can an employer contribute to a Trump Account?

Up to $2,500 per employee per year excluded from income, across all of the employee's children combined. It counts toward each child's $5,000 annual limit.

Are employer Trump Account contributions taxable?

They are excluded from the employee's federal income (up to $2,500). Proposed regulations say they are still wages for FICA and FUTA unless another exclusion applies. They are taxed to the child when withdrawn.

Which companies match Trump Account contributions?

Treasury's August 11, 2026 release cites Visa, State Street and Franklin Templeton matching the $1,000, Vanguard offering a $1,500 employer contribution, Chime offering a match, and Kraken sponsoring Wyoming babies born in 2026. Over 50 companies have committed.

Can I contribute pre-tax from my paycheck?

Yes, if your employer adds Trump Account contributions to its Section 125 cafeteria plan, for your dependent's account (not your own).

Is the $2,500 per child?

No. It is per employee, so two children share one $2,500 exclusion.

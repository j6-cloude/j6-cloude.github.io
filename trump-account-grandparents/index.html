Can Grandparents Contribute to a Trump Account?

By j6 · Updated October 28, 2026 · Facts from IRS and Treasury sources (listed below) · Not tax advice

Short answer: yes. Grandparents, aunts, uncles and friends can all add money. The parent who manages the account sends them a link or QR code from the app. They pay by bank account or debit card. Checks are not accepted. Everyone shares one limit: $5,000 per child per year in total.

How a grandparent adds money

  1. The parent claims the account first. Gifts cannot go in before that.
  2. The parent opens the child's account in the app, taps the QR code icon, then Share link, and sends it by text or email.
  3. The grandparent opens the link and pays from a bank account or debit card. Bank transfers take up to 5 business days; debit cards are usually instant.

Only the parent (the responsible party) can log in. Grandparents cannot see the balance in the app.

Can a grandparent open the account?

Sometimes. Form 4547 lists who may open an account: a legal guardian first, then a parent, then an adult sibling, then a grandparent. In most families the parent claims it. Only one person can manage it, and the first to finish the claim gets control.

The $5,000 limit is shared

If parents add $3,000 and a grandparent adds $2,500, the app declines anything past $5,000 for that year. Employer money also counts. The $1,000 Treasury seed and charity gifts do not count.

Gift tax: do grandparents need to file?

Usually not. IRS Rev. Proc. 2026-25 gives a safe harbor. A cash gift to a Trump Account counts as a normal gift under the yearly exclusion ($19,000 per person in 2026) if all your gifts to that grandchild in the year stay at or under $19,000 and you do not otherwise have to file a gift tax return. If you give more than $19,000 to that grandchild in total, you file Form 709 and the Trump Account gifts must be reported too.

Is it a good gift?

It grows untaxed until 18, and the grandparent's money becomes basis, so it comes out tax-free later. The trade-offs: it is locked until 18, then the grandchild controls it. For college, a 529 may be better. See Trump Account vs 529 and FAFSA effects.

Frequently asked questions

Can grandparents contribute to a Trump Account?

Yes. Anyone can contribute once the account is claimed. The parent shares a contribution link or QR code from the app, and the grandparent pays by bank account or debit card.

How much can a grandparent put in a Trump Account?

All contributors together can add up to $5,000 per child per year (2026), including any employer money. Contributions over the limit are declined.

Can grandparents open a Trump Account?

A grandparent is on the list of authorized individuals in the Form 4547 instructions, after a legal guardian, parent and adult sibling. Only one person can be the responsible party.

Do grandparents have to file a gift tax return?

Generally no. Under Rev. Proc. 2026-25, cash Trump Account gifts are treated as present-interest gifts if the donor's total gifts to that grandchild for the year are no more than the $19,000 annual exclusion (2026) and no gift tax return is otherwise required.

Can I mail a check to a Trump Account?

No. The official help center says checks are not accepted; contributions are made electronically.

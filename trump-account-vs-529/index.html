Trump Account vs 529 Plan

Written by j6 · Updated October 27, 2026 · Based on IRS/Treasury guidance · Not tax advice

Short answer: if college is the main goal, a 529 usually wins because qualified education withdrawals are tax-free, while Trump Account growth is taxed as income. Always grab the free $1,000 seed (and any employer money) first; then a 529 for education; a Trump Account makes more sense for long-term, non-education wealth.

Which should you fund first?

  1. Elect the $1,000 seed on Form 4547 if your child was born 2025–2028 — it costs nothing.
  2. Take any employer Trump Account contribution; it is excluded from your income up to $2,500.
  3. For expected college costs, fund a 529 (check your state's deduction).
  4. Extra long-term money: a Trump Account, or a custodial Roth IRA if the child has earned income (see comparison).

Frequently asked questions

Is a Trump Account better than a 529?

For college savings, a 529 is usually better because qualified education withdrawals are tax-free. A Trump Account is better for free money (the $1,000 seed, employer contributions) and long-term, non-education investing.

Can I have both a Trump Account and a 529?

Yes. Nothing prevents a child from having both.

Can Trump Account money be used for college?

After the account becomes IRA-like at 18, IRA rules apply: withdrawals for qualified higher-education expenses avoid the 10% early-withdrawal penalty, but the taxable part is still subject to income tax.

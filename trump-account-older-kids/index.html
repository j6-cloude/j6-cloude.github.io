Trump Accounts for Older Kids

By j6 · Updated October 28, 2026 · Facts from IRS and Treasury sources (listed below) · Not tax advice

Short answer: yes, older kids get a Trump Account too. Any child under 18 with a Social Security number can have one. Treasury began opening them automatically on Oct 1, 2026. Older kids do not get the $1,000 (that is only for U.S. citizens born 2025 to 2028). Many kids born 2016 to 2024 got $250 from the Dell gift. Starting late still helps, but there are fewer years to grow.

How long can you add money?

Only until Dec 31 of the year your child turns 17. A child who is 14 now has about 4 more years of deposits.

Is a Trump Account the best place for a teen's money?

  • If your teen has a job, a custodial Roth IRA is usually better: growth can be tax-free.
  • If the money is for college in a few years, a 529 is usually better.
  • A Trump Account still makes sense for employer money, charity gifts, and long-term money with no earned income.
  • At 18 your child controls it. Talk with them about leaving it invested.

Frequently asked questions

Can older kids get a Trump Account?

Yes. Any child with a valid SSN can have one if it is set up before the year they turn 18. In 2026 that means children born in 2009 or later.

Do older kids get the $1,000?

No. The $1,000 pilot contribution is only for U.S. citizens born January 1, 2025 through December 31, 2028.

What is the $250 for older kids?

A private gift from Michael and Susan Dell for children born 2016–2024 in ZIP codes with median family income of $117,300 or less (or on military bases). It was deposited automatically by October 9, 2026.

Is it worth opening a Trump Account for a teenager?

It can be, especially for employer or charity money, but a custodial Roth IRA (if the teen has earned income) or a 529 (for college) often has better tax treatment.

When do contributions stop?

After December 31 of the year the child turns 17.
